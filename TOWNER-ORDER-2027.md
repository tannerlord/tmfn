# 2027 stock order: Towner State Nursery

Source: Towner State Nursery 2027 catalog (ND Forest Service).
https://www.ndsu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Towner_State_Nursery_2027-Web-Catalog_0.pdf

## Read this before you order

The catalog says:

> "Nursery stock is intended for conservation plantings and shall not be used in ornamental or landscape plantings."

Potting Towner seedlings and selling them as $20 yard trees is very likely what that line forbids. The catalog doesn't mention resale either way. Ask Towner before you send the order. Call 701-537-5636 or email tnursery@srt.com and say plainly what you do: "I grow seedlings on in gallon pots and sell them to landowners for windbreaks and yards. Can I buy from you for that?"

- **If they say yes:** get it in writing (an email is fine), then order the list below.
- **If they say no or only conservation:** buy the same species from a private wholesale grower that sells for resale. Lincoln-Oakes Nurseries in Bismarck is listed as a private wholesale nursery that sells to others for resale: (701) 223-8575. Expect higher prices than Towner. The quantities below still work as a plan.

Also: North Dakota requires a nursery license to sell nursery stock. It costs $50 a year, expires December 31, and growers are inspected each year between May and September. If you don't already have one, apply at ole.ndda.nd.gov before you sell in 2027.

### Why this mix

- **The two spruces are the biggest line and the biggest stock.** They're what most people here ask for, and a 16–24" spruce looks like a real tree in a gallon pot the first summer. The 8–15" size costs half as much but takes another year or two to look worth $20. Spruce is slow, so buying size is worth it.
- **Ponderosa pine 2-2 (12–18")** is the largest pine Towner offers, for the same reason. It's only 50 because pine sells less than spruce here.
- **Juneberry gets 100.** It's native, fruits, and is the easiest "edible landscaping" sale.
- **Containers for the rest.** Oak, aspen, larch, juneberry and buffaloberry come only as container stock. For the junipers, containers cost $0.15 more than bare-root and transplant into pots with fewer losses.
- **Bare-root 2-0 or 1-0 for chokecherry, plum and lilac.** These are cheap and fast, and bare-root shrubs pot up easily.
- **50 of everything else.** That's the smallest lot. It tests demand without leaving you stuck with a pile of one species.

### What I left out

| Species | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Meyer spruce, Norway spruce, Douglas fir | They overlap the two spruces, and customers don't ask for them by name. Norway and Douglas fir are less reliable in Zone 3. |
| Scotch pine | Pine wilt hits it on the Plains. It's a harder premium sale than ponderosa. |
| Caragana, golden and laurel-leaf willow, common chokecherry, sandcherry | Cheap shelterbelt commodities with low retail value. They fit the 2028–29 wholesale seedling plan better than $20 pots. |

## Pick up, don't ship

UPS in-state for 701–800 trees is **$285**. The catalog also lists **$60 per 50** for 16–24" spruce and 12–18" 2-2 pine. Applied to this order, that's another $300. So shipping costs **$285–585**. Pickup costs one trip from Bottineau to Towner, roughly 40–50 miles each way, plus a morning. Stock is usually ready April through the end of May. Call in early spring to book a pickup date.

Don't send payment until Towner confirms the order. They take check or credit card.

## Rough economics

These are planning assumptions, not quotes.

| | Likely | Best |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Towner stock | $1,343 | $1,343 |
| Gallon pots, potting mix, fertilizer, irrigation parts | ~$1,300 | ~$1,300 |
| **Cash in** | **~$2,650** | **~$2,650** |
| Loss after potting | 15% (≈680 sellable) | 10% (≈720 sellable) |
| Sold in 2027 at $20 | 300 → $6,000 | 600 → $12,000 |
| **Net cash, 2027** | **~$3,350** | **~$9,350** |
| Carried into 2028 | ~380 trees, bigger | ~120 trees |

- **Break-even** is about 135 trees sold.
- **Time:** expect 15–20 hours to pot 800 trees, then watering all summer. Value that at your $20 an hour when you compare this to web work.
- **Weak spot 1, small stock:** a 6–15" oak or juneberry is an easy sell to a windbreak buyer but a hard sell at $20 to a yard buyer in July. Consider holding the small stuff until fall 2027 or spring 2028, or pricing stock under 18" at $15.
- **Weak spot 2, winter:** roots in gallon pots are far less hardy than roots in the ground. Unsold trees need to be heeled into the ground or mulched in a block before freeze-up, or the carryover inventory dies.
